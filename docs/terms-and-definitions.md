# Terms and definitions

ISO and IEC maintain terminological databases for use in standardization
at the following addresses:

- ISO Online browsing platform: available at
  [https://www.iso.org/obp](https://www.iso.org/obp)
- IEC Electropedia: available at
  [http://www.electropedia.org/](http://www.electropedia.org/)

**component**

part of a system

Note 1 to entry: A component can be hardware or software and can be
subdivided into other components. Component refers to a part of a whole,
such as a component of a software product or
a component of a software identification tag.
The terms module, component, and unit are often used interchangeably or
defined to be subelements of one another in different ways depending
upon the context. The relationship of these terms is not standardized.
A component can be independently managed or not from
the end-user or administrator's point of view (from SEVOCAB).

[SOURCE: IEEE 1012-2024, 3.1, modified — "One part that makes up a system" has been modified to satisfy the substitutability principle]

**computer hardware**

all or part of the physical components of an information system

[SOURCE: ISO/IEC 2382:2015, modified — leveraged from definition of 'hardware']

**computer program**

combination of computer instructions and data definitions
that enable computer hardware to perform computational or control functions

[SOURCE: ISO/IEC/IEEE 24765:2017]

**firmware**

computer programs and data stored in hardware —
typically in read-only memory (ROM) or programmable read-only memory (PROM) —
such that the programs and data cannot be dynamically written or modified
during execution of the programs

[SOURCE: CNSSI 4009-2015, leveraged from RFC 4949]

**hardware**

material physical components of a system

[SOURCE: CNSSI 4009-2015, modified — the leading article "The" has been removed to satisfy the substitutability principle]

**information system**

organized assembly of computing and communication resources and procedures
— equipment and services, together with their supporting infrastructure,
facilities, and personnel — that create, collect, record, process, store,
transport, retrieve, display, disseminate, control, or dispose of
information to accomplish a specified set of functions

[SOURCE: CNSSI 4009-2015, leveraged from RFC 4949, modified — the leading article "An" has been removed to satisfy the substitutability principle]

**interface**

point at which two or more logical, physical, or both,
system elements or software system elements meet and act on or communicate with each other

[SOURCE: ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2026, 3.1.33; ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2023, 3.20]

**persona**

representation of a type of user that includes
a concise summary of the characteristics of the user
that is most informative to the design or illustrative of specific user
requirements

Note 1 to entry: A persona typically includes behavior patterns, goals,
skills, attitudes, and environment, with a few fictional personal details
to make the persona a realistic character.

[SOURCE: ISO/IEC 25063:2014]

**profile**

scope of usage for SPDX targeting support for particular use cases and scenarios

Note 1 to entry: A profile identifies which particular SPDX namespaces, classes, and properties
it leverages, along with any custom constraints unique to its use.

**software**

computer programs (which are stored in and executed by computer hardware)
and associated data (which also is stored in the hardware)
that may be dynamically written or modified during execution

[SOURCE: CNSSI 4009-2015, leveraged from RFC 4949]

**stakeholder**

individual or organization having a right, share, claim, or interest
in a system or in its possession of characteristics
that meet their needs and expectations

[SOURCE: ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288:2015, via NIST SP 800-160v1r1]

**system**

organized assembly of resources and procedures
united and regulated by interaction or interdependence
to accomplish a set of specific functions

Note 1 to entry: Systems also include specialized systems such as
industrial/process controls systems,
telephone switching and private branch exchange (PBX) systems,
and environmental control systems.

[SOURCE: CNSSI 4009-2015, modified — the leading term "Any" has been removed to satisfy the substitutability principle]
